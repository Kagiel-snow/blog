Wanting to learn Japanese doesn't have to start with a grand plan. Maybe there's one line in a game you'd love to understand, a word in a song that keeps catching your ear, or a conversation you'd like to have on a future trip. That feels like plenty of reason to begin.

What I'd recommend is a routine that leaves some room to breathe. A huge timetable can turn something you enjoy into another thing to feel behind on.

## Make a little space—perhaps twenty minutes

Spend a few minutes recalling yesterday's words. Listen to a short piece of audio, check its transcript, then say it aloud a few times. At the end, change one part of a sentence to make it about your own life. Twenty minutes is just a convenient shape, not a rule; on a busy day, doing one part is fine.

If kana still feels unfamiliar, take time to connect the written characters with their sounds. Once reading feels easier, choose short sentences close to your current level. For review, try covering the answer and recalling it rather than relying on the comfortable feeling of recognising it. Meeting a forgotten word again doesn't have to count as a defeat.

## Fewer resources, a little more listening

One place I'd suggest starting is the Japan Foundation's *Irodori: Japanese for Life in Japan*. It organises learning around everyday situations and includes audio, so you can work with one small exchange at a time. Staying with a resource for a while seems kinder than endlessly collecting new courses.

Anime and songs can be lovely sources of motivation too. I just wouldn't assume a character's dramatic way of speaking belongs in a conversation with a stranger. Enjoy recognising a phrase, then look at how it fits into ordinary conversation.

There doesn't need to be a score at the end of every session. Hearing one more word clearly, or saying one sentence out loud, is enough to make today a little different from yesterday.

Explore the material: [Irodori: Japanese for Life in Japan](https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp/).
